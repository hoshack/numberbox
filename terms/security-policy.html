Vulnerability Disclosure & Security Testing Policy

Last updated: September 2026 | Framework: ISO/IEC 29147, Budapest Convention & CFAA Safe Harbor

1. International Legal Framework & Authorization

Under international cybercrime jurisprudence and international treaties, accessing and testing computerized systems is categorized as unlawful strictly when performed without authorization or without legal rights:

Council of Europe Budapest Convention on Cybercrime (ETS No. 185, Article 2 - Illegal Access):
"Each Party shall adopt such legislative and other measures as may be necessary to establish as criminal offences under its domestic law, when committed intentionally, the access to the whole or any part of a computer system without right."

Legal Application:
The legal core of cybercrime statutes universally revolves around the phrase "without right". Because the owner, administrators, and operators of NumberBox provide this explicit, written, public authorization and irrevocable consent, any security research, penetration testing, and vulnerability evaluation conducted in good faith within our scope constitutes authorized access under international law and the Budapest Convention.

Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA - 18 U.S.C. § 1030) & DOJ Policy on Good-Faith Research (2022):
"The Department of Justice does not prosecute good-faith security researchers conducting research solely for the purpose of good-faith testing, investigation, and/or correction of a security flaw or vulnerability."

This document serves as an open Safe Harbor commitment waiving anti-circumvention claims (e.g. DMCA Section 1201) and protecting researchers against civil and criminal legal action.

3. Safe Harbor Commitment (No Litigation)

If you conduct vulnerability research in good faith and in compliance with this policy, we commit to the following:

  • We will not initiate legal action or file criminal complaints against you for accidental, good-faith violations of security perimeters or testing within scope.
  • We consider your research activities authorized under CFAA (18 U.S.C. § 1030) and the Budapest Cybercrime Convention.
  • We will acknowledge your contributions, validate your findings promptly, and reward qualifying security discoveries.
